Most research you can read for free exists to sell you something. It is written after the position is taken, it reports the wins, and it never states in advance what would have counted as being wrong. That is not analysis; it is marketing with charts.

This page describes what published research here has to do instead. It is deliberately the first thing on the research index, because a standard that is not written down is not a standard.

## The authority boundary

The investment mandate is private. Published research can challenge a claim, but
it cannot set conviction, rewrite the mandate, or authorize execution.

Evidence is grouped by the job it performs: cycle and risk, liquidity, market
structure, frontier technology, and protocol fundamentals. These are analytical
lenses, not public endorsements or delegated authority.

No single input may supply more than 25% of an evidence packet. Every consequential
claim requires at least two independent checks and a written falsifier. A claim that
survives only one lens is weaker and is labelled that way.

## The four questions

Every published report answers these, in this order.

**1. What regime are we in?** Cycle, liquidity, breadth, dominance, credit, and
energy conditions define the risk budget before a narrative earns attention.

**2. Is the theme becoming economically real?** Retained users, utilization,
settlement volume, and application revenue matter more than an attractive story.

**3. Where does value accrue?** Real fees, token capture, net supply, capital
intensity, and exit rights determine whether adoption benefits the asset being studied.

**4. What would prove this wrong?** Written down before the outcome is known,
specific enough to be checked, and dated. A thesis without a falsifier cannot be scored.

## What is withheld

Current holdings, sizing, and exact capital figures do not appear in published research.

This is not coyness. A public real-time book invites front-running. The live architecture feed is a separate contract: it reports how the system is operating and rejects wallet identifiers and capital fields at ingest. The separate capital surface remains banded, while current holdings and sizes are simply absent from anonymous responses.

What that leaves is the part that was always the valuable part — the method, and closed calls scored against falsifiers committed in advance. A framework you can apply to your own holdings is worth more than a ticker you have no way to check.

## What is not here

Nothing on this site is investment advice, an offer, or a solicitation, and none of it is personalised to anyone's circumstances.

The research describes how a particular system reasons about assets. It does not tell you what to do, because whoever wrote it does not know your position, your timeline, or your tolerance for being wrong — and anyone publishing to a general audience who implies otherwise is not being straight with you.

## Why the index may be empty

Research publishes only when a report is explicitly marked for publication. Nothing is swept in automatically.

That gate exists because the underlying research vault also contains machine-generated notes, and some of them are unreliable — connection-mining output that has invented tickers and fabricated events that never happened. Those files are useful as raw material for a human to sift. They are not fit to publish, and no automated quality score is trusted to tell the difference.

So the index shows only what has been deliberately released. If that set is small, the page will look sparse. An empty page is a much better failure mode than a confident wrong one, and a platform that would rather show you nothing than show you something it has not checked is telling you something useful about how it treats everything else.
